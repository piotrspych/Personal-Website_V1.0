The challenge

An aging frigate, a fixed dock window and no room for slippage

HMCS Toronto is one of the Royal Canadian Navy’s Halifax-class frigates, ships in service since the 1990s that must be periodically docked for hull, mechanical and systems maintenance to stay mission-ready. Each docking work period is planned years in advance and ties up dock space, contractor trades and naval resources for a fixed window.

Our approach

A three-phase plan, from scope to undocking

We treated the docking period as one connected system: every work item, interference and dependency was mapped before the ship entered the dock, so decisions in the yard could be made quickly and with the full picture.

What made the difference

Three lessons for complex maintenance programs

  1. Map interferences before the ship docks. Knowing where work items clash ahead of time prevents rework and idle trades once the clock is running.
  2. One baseline, every stakeholder. A single shared schedule between the Navy, procurement and the shipyard keeps decisions fast when conditions change.
  3. Plan for growth work. Building contingency into scope and schedule turns surprises into managed changes instead of delays.
